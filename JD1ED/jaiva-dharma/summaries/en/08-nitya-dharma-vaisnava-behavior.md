<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:bold;color:#000000;">Chapter 8 – Nitya-dharma & Vaiṣṇava Behavior</p>

<!-- p001 -->
By the pond Śrī Gorā-hrada a young Vaiṣṇava from Baragāchī asked how one who has taken shelter of vaiṣṇava-dharma should behave toward others. The reply came from Haridāsa Bābājī, who was almost a hundred years old. The ten marks of religious life according to Manu (determination, forgiveness, self-control, cleanliness, truthfulness, and others) apply to people in general, but none of them points to hari-bhajana. Those who have accepted the path of bhakti are of three kinds: kaniṣṭha, madhyama, and uttama.

<!-- p002 -->
The kaniṣṭha worships the Deity with faith, but does not serve the bhaktas or other beings (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.2.47). His faith is customary and his bhakti only a shadow of bhakti; he cannot recognize a true bhakta and behaves toward others according to general moral principles. He advances quickly when the association of bhaktas becomes predominant in his life.

<!-- p003 -->
The madhyama Vaiṣṇava (11.2.46) behaves in four ways, which are part of nitya-dharma. He loves Kṛṣṇa with pure bhakti, free from other desires and not covered by jñāna and karma. He is a friend to the pure bhaktas: he associates with them and serves three kinds of Vaiṣṇavas – one who has uttered kṛṣṇa-nāma once, one who chants it constantly, and one whose mere sight inspires others to chant. He is merciful to the ignorant who have not been contaminated by unauthorized doctrines: he awakens their faith and their taste for śrī-nāma and protects them from bad association. And he neglects the inimical – those who do not believe in Īśvara, consider Him a mere force of nature, deny Him form, deny the eternal subordination of the jīvas, or are devoid of mercy. Neglect (upekṣā) does not mean refusing them ordinary social dealings or help in distress, but avoiding their association in spiritual matters.

<!-- p004 -->
The uttama-bhakta has attained prema; he sees Kṛṣṇa in all and all in Kṛṣṇa (11.2.45) and does not distinguish between Vaiṣṇava and non-Vaiṣṇava. To consider the level of Vaiṣṇavas and to serve them is therefore the duty of the madhyama; if he neglects it, he becomes an offender. Nityānanda dāsa, who admitted a remnant of desire for honor, was warned by Bābājī that the desire for fame is the most pernicious enemy of the Vaiṣṇavas. One's level is not measured by whether one is a householder or an ascetic, but by one's progress in bhakti. According to Śikṣāṣṭaka (3), one should be free from pride and offer everyone due respect. Other dharmas are either steps toward vaiṣṇava-dharma, to be respected according to their purity, or distortions of it; a Vaiṣṇava should bear no enmity toward their followers. Preaching is a duty, but śrī-kṛṣṇa-nāma is not given to the unqualified.
