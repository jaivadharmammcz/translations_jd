<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:bold;color:#000000;">Chapter 3 – Naimittika-dharma is to be relinquished</p>

<!-- p001 -->
One night, while Sannyāsī Mahārāja was chanting hari-nāma, the transcendental form of Śrī Māyāpura appeared before him. In the morning he threw his ekadaṇḍa staff into the river, put on tulasī beads and tilaka, and Paramahaṁsa Bābājī named him Vaiṣṇava dāsa. That afternoon one of those present, the brāhmaṇa and zamindar Kālīdāsa Lāhirī, asked: The dharma-śāstras declare the brāhmaṇas to be the highest caste and rites such as sandhyā-vandanā to be their nitya-karma; why is the behavior of the Vaiṣṇavas contrary to them? Vaiṣṇava dāsa replied.

<!-- p002 -->
Human nature has two tendencies: vaidhī, which follows the rules of śāstra, and rāgānugā, which follows the soul's spontaneous attraction to Kṛṣṇa. Rāgānugā is the unalloyed nature of the jīva, but jīvas bound by māyā do not know it, and it is awakened only by the mercy of sādhus; therefore Bhagavān mercifully manifested the śāstras and the path of rules. Śāstra gives different instructions according to eligibility (adhikāra) for karma, jñāna, and prema. Karma that does not lead to jñāna, and karma and jñāna that do not lead to bhakti, are cheating processes; in reality they are steps on a single staircase leading to the temple of bhakti. For the proper performance of karma the śāstras established varṇāśrama-dharma; varṇa is determined by nature, qualities, and activities, not by birth alone.

<!-- p003 -->
The words nitya and naimittika are applied to karma only in a conventional, figurative sense. Sandhyā-vandanā is called nitya-karma because it remotely points toward nitya-dharma; the only real nitya-karma of the jīva is kṛṣṇa-prema. Varṇāśrama, aṣṭāṅga-yoga, sāṅkhya-jñāna, and tapasya are all naimittika, because the jīva would not need them if he were not bound – and the same applies to the superiority of the brāhmaṇas and to their sannyāsa. According to Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (7.9.10), a bhakta born among dog-eaters is superior to a brāhmaṇa with the twelve qualities who is averse to Bhagavān. Of those who are spiritually unawakened, the brāhmaṇas are the best; those who are spiritually awakened are called Vaiṣṇavas. Their behavior is different, yet it does not oppose the aim of the smṛti.

<!-- p004 -->
Naimittika-dharma is incomplete, because it is only a means to an end; adulterated, because it yields extraneous results such as the brāhmaṇa's pride, mystic powers, mukti, and bhukti; impermanent, because it changes with every birth; and fit to be rejected. Vaiṣṇava-dharma is the nitya-dharma of the jīva. A Vaiṣṇava accepts the rules of the śāstras when they are favorable to his hari-bhajana and rejects them when they are unfavorable. Lāhirī Mahāśaya accepted Vaiṣṇava dāsa in his heart as his guru.
