<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:bold;color:#000000;">Chapter 4 – Vaiṣṇava-dharma is Nitya-dharma</p>

<!-- p001 -->
When Lāhirī Mahāśaya was frightened by a snake at his door, Vaiṣṇava dāsa Bābājī reminded him that the body is not eternal and that nothing can harm it without Kṛṣṇa's will; a mind agitated by fear cannot be fixed on the lotus feet of Śrī Hari. He then explained that two dharmas go by the name vaiṣṇava-dharma. The pure one (śuddha) is one, has four divisions according to rasa – dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya, and mādhurya – and is nitya-dharma. The adulterated one (viddha) is mixed either with karma, where Viṣṇu is merely part of the ritual, or with jñāna, where five deities are worshiped (pañcopāsanā) as a means to the featureless brahma.

<!-- p002 -->
People have three tendencies toward the Absolute Truth: toward brahma, toward Paramātmā, and toward Bhagavān. Bhagavān is the highest tattva and the basis of the other two (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.2.11). The dharmas that arise from the tendencies toward brahma and Paramātmā are naimittika; only unalloyed bhāgavata-dharma is eternal. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu taught three fundamental principles: sambandha (one's relationship with Bhagavān), abhidheya (the means), and prayojana (the goal).

<!-- p003 -->
Sambandha includes Bhagavān, the jīvas, and the material world. Bhagavān is one, endowed with all energies; the effulgence of His limbs is brahma, His partial expansion in the world is Paramātmā, in Vaikuṇṭha He is Nārāyaṇa, and in Goloka Vṛndāvana He is Śrī Kṛṣṇa. His form is sac-cid-ānanda and has nothing to do with matter. His energy is manifested as internal (cit), marginal (jīva), and māyā. The jīvas are atomic entities of pure consciousness, either liberated or bound; māyā is the external energy, the bodies are her creation, and the universe is a prison, not the jīva's eternal home. Without sambandha-jñāna no one becomes a pure Vaiṣṇava; the bhāva of those who consider themselves one with brahma is only a pretense. No learning is required for this – the guru imparts this knowledge by his words and conduct.

<!-- p004 -->
Abhidheya is sādhana-bhakti. Bhakti has three stages: sādhana, bhāva, and prema. Sādhana has nine forms (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 7.5.23) and two kinds: vaidhī, impelled by the rules of śāstra, and rāgānugā, impelled by spontaneous love. Bābājī determined vaidhī-bhakti for Lāhirī and instructed him to chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra without offenses, for there is no difference between the name and its possessor, and nāma-bhajana includes all nine forms. Lāhirī began to chant two lakhs of names daily and became a Vaiṣṇava. Prayojana is kṛṣṇa-prema: from steady sādhana bhāva arises, and fully developed bhāva is prema – the eternal function, wealth, and goal of the jīva.
