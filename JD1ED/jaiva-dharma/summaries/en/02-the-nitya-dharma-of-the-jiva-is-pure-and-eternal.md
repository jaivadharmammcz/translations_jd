<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:bold;color:#000000;">Chapter 2 – The Nitya-dharma of the Jīva is Pure and Eternal</p>

<!-- p001 -->
The next day Sannyāsī Ṭhākura asked: If the jīva is infinitesimal, how can his eternal dharma be complete and pure? And if his function is formed at the moment he is constituted, how can it be eternal? Bābājī replied that the jīvas emanate from Kṛṣṇa like sparks from a fire, and just as each spark has the power of the whole fire, each jīva can display the full function of consciousness. The nitya-dharma of the jīva is prema, transcendental love for Kṛṣṇa, whose nature is service.

<!-- p002 -->
The jīva exists in two states: the pure (śuddha-avasthā) and the conditioned (baddha-avasthā). In the pure state he considers himself Kṛṣṇa's servant; in contact with māyā his identity is covered by the subtle and gross bodies, and his prema is manifested pervertedly as happiness and distress, attachment and aversion, and sense enjoyment. The dharma of the conditioned state is naimittika. The religions of the world may be divided into anitya-dharma, which accepts neither Īśvara nor the eternality of the soul; naimittika-dharma, which accepts them but seeks Īśvara's mercy only by provisional methods; and nitya-dharma, which strives for the service of Bhagavān out of unalloyed prema. Nitya-dharma is one, whatever it is called in different countries; its ideal is vaiṣṇava-dharma as taught by Śrī Caitanya, and the degree of vaiṣṇava-dharma in any religion is the measure of its purity.

<!-- p003 -->
Asked about advaita-siddhi, Bābājī first offered obeisances to Śaṅkarācārya: he is Mahādeva-Śaṅkara himself, the guru of the Vaiṣṇavas, who restored the authority of the Vedas when śūnyavāda prevailed and laid the foundation on which the Vaiṣṇava ācāryas built. Up to the point of liberation there is great agreement between them; Śaṅkara was merely silent about what the jīva attains after liberation. Prema, however, requires the separate existence of two transcendental entities. Māyāvāda, which regards the states of prema as illusory, is therefore opposed to the Vedas according to the Vaiṣṇavas.

<!-- p004 -->
When the sannyāsī wished to give up his dress, Bābājī said that he gives no instructions about external dress: when the dharma of the heart is purified, the external is set right by itself, and one should not adopt monkey renunciation (markaṭa-vairāgya). To the second question he replied that the “creation” of the jīva refers to material time; in spiritual time (cit-kāla), which is an eternal present, both the jīva and his dharma are eternal. This cannot be understood by logic, but by spiritual meditation. He instructed him to chant hari-nāma constantly, for nāma-saṅkīrtana is the best of the nine forms of bhakti and, chanted without offenses, bestows the treasure of prema. That day Sannyāsī Ṭhākura experienced a taste (ruci) for hari-nāma.
