<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:bold;color:#000000;">Chapter 9 – Nitya-dharma, Material Science & civilization</p>

<!-- p001 -->
After several years in Śrī Godruma the heart of Lāhirī Mahāśaya became purified; he understood that what is essential is detachment in the heart, not the external dress of renunciation, and he received the name Advaita dāsa. He was visited by his childhood friend Digambara Caṭṭopādhyāya, a wealthy official, a worshiper of Durgā, and an expert in the tantras. Digambara claimed that the servants of the goddess are more civilized than the Vaiṣṇavas, enjoy the happiness afforded by material science, and that there is no salvation without Mother Nistāriṇī, the primordial power.

<!-- p002 -->
Digambara defined civilization as polite speech, respectable dress, and agreeable conduct. Advaita dāsa replied that life is short and the only duty of a human being is to worship Śrī Hari sincerely. The word “civilization” has become a name for polished deceit that conceals depravity; true civilization, free from sin and deceit, consists of truthfulness and simplicity and is found only among the Vaiṣṇavas. The so-called progress of Kali-yuga is merely an increase of sin.

<!-- p003 -->
Knowledge is of two kinds: paramārthika, concerning eternal truth, and laukika, concerning the transient world. Only the latter is increasing, and with it people neglect spiritual truth. The jīvas are like travelers at an inn; material knowledge should serve only to secure one's livelihood. Military science, medicine, astronomy, and chemistry are all knowledge of material objects, with which the jīva has no eternal connection and which has no significance for his nitya-dharma. Yet the Vaiṣṇavas do not condemn those who strive for material progress: everyone works according to his tendency, and thus the whole world unknowingly serves the Vaiṣṇavas.

<!-- p004 -->
Mother Nistāriṇī is viṣṇu-māyā, the external energy of Śrī Hari. Śakti is not an independent object but a power inherent in an object, and she acts according to Bhagavān's desire. Bhagavān has one śakti: on the spiritual plane she is cit-śakti, Rādhikā; on the material plane she is māyā, Durgā; the jīvas are the marginal śakti between them (Bhagavad-gītā 7.4–6). The true śāktas are therefore the Vaiṣṇavas, who are under the shelter of Śrī Rādhikā; those attached only to māyā are merely materialists. Digambara became angry: nothing remains after death, so one should enjoy. Advaita dāsa replied that happiness is peace of mind, and the thirst for enjoyment only grows by enjoying. He had not given up the company of cultured people but gained it; he gives up the company of the fallen, for by associating with them one's good qualities fade (Hari-bhakti-vilāsa). Digambara refused prasāda and took his leave.
